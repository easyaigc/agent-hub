# P9 native coordinator fixture correction and binding review

## Finding and change

The coordinator runs its jobs on Main. The shipping `NativeChatNotificationHttp` recovery methods switch to `Dispatchers.IO` internally. The native fake recovery transport previously called `journal.view` in `recoverySubmit` on the caller thread. That journal correctly rejects Main access. The recovery controller then correctly keeps the original operation unknown after an uncertain submit, so these positive coordinator scenarios cannot reach the feed or presentation waits.

The native recovery fixture is now extracted unchanged in scope into `NativeChatNotificationRecoveryFixture.kt`, shared by the original fifteen recovery scenarios and all twenty-one coordinator scenarios. Its four named recovery methods dispatch to IO, matching the shipping transport contract. Its cross-thread observation lists/maps use concurrent collections. The coordinator fixture's read-only v1 status method also dispatches its preference read to IO. No assertion, wait timeout, expected event, journal guard, production catch, operation-ID rule or actual transport is relaxed. All twenty-one coordinator methods and fifteen recovery methods remain present.

The JVM runner compiles that same native fixture source, rather than a copied replacement, and supplies its explicit memory-storage and synthetic Looper ports. The new regression reserves the immutable original on IO, invokes the fake submit from a synthetic Main caller, requires its applied receipt, settles that same original on IO, and then requires synchronized feed processing with exactly one submit, one feed and one delivered event. Before the fix it exits nonzero with `Recovery journal requires IO` at the native fixture's `recoverySubmit`; after the fix all sixty auxiliary cases pass. This is evidence for the test defect, not a physical Android rerun.
